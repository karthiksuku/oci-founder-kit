# Go to market with Oracle

The revenue side of the value case. Oracle's Startup Growth Engine offers
credits, technical guidance and, for selected companies with commercially
ready products, a route to market through Oracle Cloud Marketplace and joint
go-to-market. This skill tells the founder whether they fit, what to prepare,
and how much of the value is real for their sales motion.

Everything here is a draft. Never submit an application, contact Oracle,
accept terms or publish a listing on the founder's behalf.

## Modes

| Mode | Use when | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Fit check | "Should we apply?" | Fit against the published criteria, blockers, verdict |
| Application draft | The founder decides to apply | Draft answers grounded in the value case, with gaps marked |
| Readiness review | Preparing for Marketplace or co-selling | `gtm-readiness.md` with the five stages and their gaps |
| Enterprise pack | Enterprise deals stall on security or residency | A list of the evidence buyers will ask for and what exists today |

## Workflow

1. **Check the facts are current.** Read `../founder-value/references/facts.md`.
   If it is more than 30 days old or the founder is in a country it does not
   cover, check the live program page and terms first.
2. **Fit check** with [references/growth-engine.md](references/growth-engine.md).
   Stop at any exclusion. Existing OCI customers may be considered with a
   substantial growth case; say exactly that.
3. **Ground the application in numbers.** Oracle reviews the proposed
   workload, commercial readiness and growth opportunity. Use the
   `founder-value` model for the workload's monthly OCI consumption at list
   price, its growth, and the customers or pilots that validate the product.
   An application with a quantified workload is easier to assess than one
   with a description.
4. **Readiness review** with
   [references/marketplace-readiness.md](references/marketplace-readiness.md).
5. **Enterprise pack** with
   [references/enterprise-readiness.md](references/enterprise-readiness.md)
   when the founder sells to larger organisations.
6. **Size the channel honestly.** If the founder wants a number:
   `qualified opportunities x win rate x first-year value`, every input
   `assumed`, presented as a range. Self-serve and consumer products usually
   get little from an enterprise channel; say so.

## Rules

- Acceptance, credit amounts, customer introductions, co-selling and
  Marketplace publication are at Oracle's discretion. Use "may", "if
  selected" and "where there is mutual fit", never "will".
- The terms forbid using Oracle's name, trademarks or logos without prior
  written authorization. No Oracle logos in decks, sites or press, and no
  "partner" or "endorsed" wording, until the founder has that authorization.
- Do not invent Oracle contacts, programme staff names, case studies or
  customer logos.
- Keep customer names and deal details out of drafts unless the founder
  supplies them and has the right to share them.

## Finish with

Fit verdict (Apply now, Apply after a named gap is closed, or Not a fit),
the gaps with owners, and the founder action that comes next, labelled
**founder action** when it leaves the machine.
