# Marketplace and joint go-to-market readiness

The program describes five go-to-market steps for selected companies:
position, prepare, publish, engage and grow. Use them as the sections of
`gtm-readiness.md`. Each step lists what to have ready and the question that
shows whether it is ready.

## 1. Position

- Target buyers by industry, size and role.
- Business value in the buyer's terms: the problem, the measurable outcome,
  the evidence.
- Two or three priority use cases.

*Ready when* a buyer in the target industry can read one paragraph and say
whether the product is for them.

## 2. Prepare

- Packaging: what the buyer gets, how it is deployed (SaaS, in the buyer's
  tenancy, or both) and what it depends on.
- Support model: hours, channels, response targets, who is on call.
- Pricing model and how it would be expressed in a Marketplace listing or a
  customer-specific private offer where those apply. Check current listing
  and transaction options with Oracle; they are not described in detail in
  the public program pages.
- Legal: terms of service, data processing agreement, security policy.
- Product readiness: install or onboarding steps a buyer can follow without
  the founder on a call.

*Ready when* a buyer could procure, deploy and get support without a custom
negotiation.

## 3. Publish

- Listing copy, screenshots, documentation and a support contact.
- Technical, commercial, legal and support requirements for publication;
  publication is subject to Oracle's program requirements.

*Ready when* the listing could be reviewed today without placeholders.

## 4. Engage

- The opportunities the founder wants Oracle to help with: industries,
  regions, account types.
- A short brief Oracle's teams could use, built from the value case.
- Willingness to join Oracle-led marketing activity where offered.

*Ready when* the founder can name the kind of account where joint work makes
sense and explain why Oracle's relationship helps there.

## 5. Grow

- Customer proof points: a measured outcome, with the customer's permission
  before it is published.
- A repeatable motion: the steps from first contact to signed contract,
  with the time each takes.

*Ready when* a second and third deal followed the same path.
